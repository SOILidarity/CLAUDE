Purpose & research questions

Purpose of the study

To describe the lived professional experiences of psychologists in Luzon on adolescent digital well-being in gaming environments.

Seven psychologists in Luzon · five practicing in Pampanga

SSOP 1

Encounter and interpret

How do psychologists in Luzon describe encountering and interpreting adolescent digital well-being in gaming environments?

SSOP 2

Weigh the factors

How do they describe the factors that shape adolescent digital well-being in gaming environments?

SSOP 3

Respond in practice

(a) Benefits and concerns they identify, (b) challenges they face, and (c) strategies they use.

Chapter 1, Statement of the Problem.

LAS Findings · contributions per theme from the coding workbook.

Interpret gaming within daily life

Found behind other concerns; read by its purpose and daily-life effects, not screen time alone.

Digital well-being is balance and control, not the absence of gaming.

Look beyond the game to the adolescent's experiences and needs.

One account differedInterviewee 4 located the risk in screen exposure itself, which she presented as her clinical hypothesis.

LAS Findings, Themes 1.1–1.4.

No factor acts alone

The stress cycle

Described independently by Interviewees 1 and 5

LAS Findings 2.1–2.5 · Cycle: Interviewee 1 (22:03); Interviewee 5 (24:45).

Understand, then regulate

5 of 7 psychologists saw benefits and concerns in the same adolescent; Interviewees 2 and 6 had not.

Challenges

Parents who see gaming as the whole problem; securing the adolescent's own commitment to change.

Strategies

1UnderstandNon-judgmental, holistic
2Agree on a planThe adolescent has a say
3Regulate graduallySmall steps, offline activities, psychoeducation
“

The goal is not to simply reduce gaming, but to understand the gamer.

Interviewee 5, 49:26

LAS Findings, Themes 3.1–3.6.

Recommendations

Practice

Practitioners

Ask about gaming at intake; assess its purpose and its effect on sleep, school and relationships.

Parents

Ask why and what they play; set limits together with the adolescent.

Schools

Sleep and gaming psychoeducation; offline activities that build belonging.

Adolescents

Notice early signs of play displacing sleep, meals or school.

Future research

Wider sampleMore of Luzon; adolescents and parents
Emerging concernsDoomscrolling, social media; screen-exposure claims
Local cultureKapampangan and other local meanings of gaming
Change over timeStress, gaming and sleep, followed longitudinally

These findings can inform school mental health services under RA 12080 (2024).
